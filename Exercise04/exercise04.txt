------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. How the threads are generated for the code with #pragma omp parallel for
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
#pragma omp parallel for creates a team of threads (OMP_NUM_THREADS = 4 or 8) and
divides the iterations of the loop directly after it, i.e. the OUTER loop over r, among
the threads. The default schedule is static: the iterations are divided into
contiguous blocks that are as equal as possible and given to the threads before the loop
starts, in order of thread number.

There are only 5 iterations of r. With 4 threads the blocks are 2, 1, 1, 1 iterations:
thread 0 takes r = 1 and 2, and threads 1, 2 and 3 take r = 3, 4 and 5. With 8 threads
there are more threads than iterations, so only 5 threads (0 to 4) get one value of r each
and threads 5, 6 and 7 get no work and stay idle.

The inner loop over k is not shared out; it is run completely (k = 1..10) by the thread
that owns that value of r, so all 10 lines for one value of r show the same thread number.
The work is therefore unbalanced with 4 threads (thread 0 does 20 iterations, the others
10) and the parallelism is limited to 5 threads, however many threads are available.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. How the threads are generated with #pragma omp parallel for collapse(2)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
collapse(2) combines the two nested loops into a single iteration space of
5 x 10 = 50 iterations (all (r, k) pairs) and divides THAT space among the threads, again
with the default static schedule: contiguous blocks of nearly equal size.

With 4 threads each thread gets 13, 13, 12 and 12 iterations; with 8 threads 7, 7, 6, 6, 6,
6, 6 and 6 iterations. Because the blocks cut across the rows, one value of r can be
split between two threads (for example r = 2 is shared by threads 0 and 1 with 4
threads, and r = 1 by threads 0 and 1 with 8 threads), and every thread (all 8) receives
work.

Comparison: without collapse the amount of parallelism is limited by the 5 iterations of
the outer loop (3 of the 8 threads idle, and uneven load with 4 threads). With collapse(2)
there are 50 iterations to share, so the load is almost perfectly balanced and all
threads are used. collapse is useful when the outer loop has few iterations compared with
the number of threads. The loops must be perfectly nested for collapse to be allowed.
